The Problem

  • Sending hundreds of invitation links over WhatsApp one by one is tiring and error-prone.
  • RSVPs are hard to total per event (for example the ceremony and the reception), so catering numbers end up inaccurate.
  • Existing templates often look worse once they are customized.
  • Changing event details still has to go through a vendor.

Product Direction

Couples pick a template, fill in their details, and publish. The layout is locked by the template and the color palettes are curated, so the result stays clean whatever goes in. The goal: an invitation ready to share in under 15 minutes after signing up.

Key Design Decisions

A few decisions from the decision log that shape the system:

  • Snapshot-based publishingDrafts can change freely, while a published version never does. Guests never see half-finished edits, and older versions can be restored.
  • Personal links: /{slug}/{token}Every guest gets their own token, so each RSVP is tied to the right guest and names cannot be faked through a URL parameter.
  • No "maybe" RSVPA "maybe" answer is useless for catering numbers, so it is not offered.
  • No public table accessAnonymous visitors only read through dedicated RPC functions, because row-level security does not hide columns.
  • Photos processed in the browserResizing, WebP conversion, and stripping EXIF data (including location) happen before upload.
